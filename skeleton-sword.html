Paused — Moves & Controls

The fight is frozen while you read. Melee is your damage — the sword out-hits every spell. Every hit in the game now carries knockback and hitstun, yours and theirs. And an ARMED skeleton holding its parry stance can turn aside anything — sword, bolt, beam, lightning, even a ground pound — because the sword is a concept. But the stance must be up when the hit lands, and it costs them when it whiffs.

Watch for: skeletons THROW their swords (evolvable SwordThrow gene) — skilled throwers lead your movement, so fake them out; a thrower must fetch its sword, takes +50% damage while unarmed, and cannot parry at all until it re-arms. Parry is a STANCE now, not a percentage: a skel must read your attack and raise its guard BEFORE the hit lands — the guard lives ~0.3s, and a stance that catches nothing lapses into a punishable OPEN! recovery (+35% damage taken). Bait them: feint, delay the swing, cancel a rekka step short, and early guards are free hits. They can RUN now: an evolvable Run gene lets a skel break into a sprint near your own buffed speed to close distance — stamina-limited, so a runner that finds nothing is WINDED and slow — and a run can convert into a run-lunge stab. Kiting with arrows or lightning is exactly the pressure that selects for it: hits landed at the end of a run pay tandem-level fitness. A thrower, early on, is also a bit likelier to sprint straight after its own fallen sword. They read hazards now: an arrow in flight is a line to step out of, and a burning fire pillar is ground to get off and route around — awareness scales with their dodge / reaction / coordination, so sloppy lineages still blunder into the fire, and selection can breed the blundering out. And they THROW EACH OTHER: at sword-throw range, a skel with a partner in arm's reach may hurl that partner at you in a high arc — the flying skel is a live hitbox (14 on connect), and even a miss delivers it, sword in hand, right next to you. The toss is an evolvable gene (SkelToss) that only pays with coordination — teamwork, selected for like everything else. What evolves is timing (ParryTiming), reads, baiting, counters to your abilities, flanking, spacing, attack choice by range, tandem coordination, and guarding fallen allies' bones — tactics first. Stats still grow, but inside hard caps: swords top out at 1.9× (no more full-screen blades), lunges at ~260 units, and no skel out-stats the boss — a wave-1000 horde wins by behaviour, chosen by a fitness-weighted roulette where tandem hits, timed parries, won baits and bone-guard hits count most and survival counts least. Nothing is forced; the wheel decides. COORDINATION, plainly: a coordinated skel (1) claims a SLOT on the ring around you — and if a partner already holds that slot, it rotates to the next gap, so the horde spreads around your facing instead of queueing up in front of you; (2) holds or hurries its own swing to land a beat after a partner's windup nearby — and a hit landed within 0.9s of a partner's hit scores as a TANDEM hit, among the highest-paying fitness there is; (3) with flank, works toward your side or back. So yes — slots, spacing and strike timing are all decided relative to each other, not just to you, and selection breeds whatever of it the wheel rewards. Bodies are tradeoffs now: SMALL skels are quick but frail, BIG ones are slow tanks — the largest, toughest late-wave skels can carry up to 206 HP, your own number, never more. Blade length trades the same way: short swords snap out fast and short, long swords wind up slowly and reach far (still capped at 1.9×). And their colours mutate like their names do — bone hue, blade tint and the colour of a burning stare all drift a few degrees per generation, rarely lurching further, so an old lineage is visibly its own.

THE HORDE FEELS ALIVE NOW: bodies are solid — skeletons crowd and shoulder you but cannot overlap you, and you cannot walk through them; a true surround is a real trap now. Their movement is a real decision set — close in, circle, orbit at blade's edge, back off, sprint to a flank slot — chosen conditionally and randomly, so no two approaches read the same. Their reaction time is human: 150–300ms, an evolvable gene — early waves visibly lag, and selection shaves the lag away over generations. Their answers to your moves are weighted dice rolls, bred per-lineage: shoot a 5-arrow spread and every skel that reacts rolls its own weights — one runs you down, one rolls, one sidesteps, one does something strange. A bait only counts when it's real: you must WHIFF an attack and the skel must shimmy out of its range with a punish window — shielding at a feint earns them nothing anymore. Watch the thin amber bar under each skel's HP: that's its energy — running and partner-throws burn it, an empty tank means winded and slow, and their runs are faster now (and can go any direction — a sprint to a flank slot reads very different from a charge). Fire leaves a burn on everything it touches. THE GENE TYPES — WHAT THE NUMBERS MEAN

Body multipliers (Speed, Size, Vitality, Range, AtkSpd, SwordLen, Lunge, StanceWin, Spacing): physical build, hard-capped. HP = (18 + wave bonus) × Vitality × Size^1.6, clamped 8–206. Size trades against speed; blade length trades reach against wind-up and re-attack cadence.

Behaviour probabilities (Reaction, Aggression, Dodge, ParryRead, Riposte, Throw, Dash, Feint, Nerve): rolled per event, never guarantees.

Tactical genes (ParryTiming, Bait, Coordination, Counter, Flank, RangeSense, BoneGuard): the decision-making selection is really for. Timing quality, true baits (a whiffed boss attack + a shimmy out of range), ring slots around you, ability answers.

Reaction time (ms): how long a skel takes to respond to anything you do — human range, 150–300ms by default. It evolves like any gene; faster reactors get a small fitness edge, so over generations the horde visibly sharpens.

Reaction weights (Run / Dodge / Lateral / Parry / Attack / Throw / Retreat): when a skel reacts, it no longer follows a script — it ROLLS among its options, weighted by these bred genes, with the Counter gene amplifying the "right" answer for the specific move you used. Every skel rolls its own dice, every time.

Run & toss: RunDrive / RunStamina drive the sprint (and its energy bar — running and throwing partners burn it); TossDrive is the partner-throw.

Identity: name letters, bone hue / saturation / lightness, blade tint, eye glow — cosmetic, drifting a visible step each generation.

Breeding: at wave clear each skel scores fitness (below), a fitness-weighted roulette picks the breeders (the "keep" fraction, tightening toward its floor as waves climb), each child inherits each gene from either parent, then mutates by the steps below.
